**Yes, suitable for eLife**, with caveats around accessibility and scope. The paper is a rigorous, high-quality methodological contribution in computational biophysics/electrophysiology that meets eLife’s emphasis on important problems, transparent evaluation, and tools that advance the field. It is not incremental; it systematically quantifies a long-standing, under-appreciated source of distortion in how kinetic information is extracted from macroscopic currents.

### Core contribution and strengths
Macroscopic ion-channel currents sit in an awkward intermediate regime: too many channels for single-channel dwell-time analysis, too few for pure deterministic Hodgkin–Huxley relaxation. Standard practice fits the deterministic mean by least squares and discards the fluctuations that still carry kinetic (and amplitude) information. Likelihood methods that attempt to use the fluctuations exist but have seen little uptake, partly because a recursive filter can track the data even when its reported uncertainty is badly wrong.

The paper’s central advance is a calibrated diagnostic apparatus, not merely another algorithm. It applies the classical Bartlett identities / information-matrix equality (score mean zero at the true parameters; score covariance equals the reported Fisher information) to a seven-rung ladder of approximations, from ordinary least squares (LSE / ILSE) through non-recursive and recursive Gaussian filters up to a boundary-conditioned interval filter (IR / MacroIR). Because the forward continuous-time Markov chain can be simulated exactly while the likelihood cannot, the author obtains ground-truth Monte-Carlo estimates of bias and of the distortion matrix \(\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}\). This turns an otherwise untestable claim into a measurable map over channel number \(N_{\mathrm{ch}}\), relative instrumental noise \(\tilde{S}\), and acquisition interval in units of the closing time constant.

Key results that emerge cleanly:
- Least-squares intervals become trustworthy only once instrumental noise dominates gating noise (an instrumental variance that scales with \(N_{\mathrm{ch}}\)); the regime in which fluctuations still inform unitary current and channel number is separated from the trustworthy-least-squares regime by 1–3 decades of noise.
- Non-recursive members under-report information by an order of magnitude because of unmodelled temporal correlation of the score.
- Intermediate recursive members (especially those conditioning on only one endpoint) can be *worse* calibrated than pure instantaneous recursion; only the full boundary-conditioned filter (IR) restores near-unity distortion over most of the explored plane.
- The distortion is anisotropic and matrix-valued; a simple scalar sample-size correction is therefore insufficient, but the sandwich covariance repairs most members.

The theoretical development is careful: uniform-window interval averages, occupancy versus interval-signal Gaussian closures, exact single-channel moments obtained from tilted semigroups or augmented matrix exponentials, and a clean separation of the two axes (window conditioning vs. recursion). Diagnostics are decomposed into sample (non-Gaussianity) and correlation components, with clear reader-facing translations (effective sample size, geometric-mean distortion \(m\), anisotropy \(a\)). Software (MacroIR for R and Python, with score and information) is promised, turning the work into a usable tool rather than a pure critique.

### Critical limitations
- **Scope**. Everything is demonstrated on a two-state scheme at open probability ½ under a single concentration jump. This is the most favourable case for the Gaussian occupancy closure and isolates approximation error cleanly, but real kinetic schemes are richer, open probabilities vary, and protocols are more complex. The author correctly notes that the two-state results supply a lower bound on error; still, readers will want to see whether the same qualitative map survives for multi-state or voltage-dependent models.
- **Density and length**. At 54 pages the manuscript is long and mathematically dense. Experimental electrophysiologists who still rely on least squares will find the ladder of filters, boundary states, and information-matrix diagnostics heavy going. The practical take-home (when least squares is safe; how to read residual autocorrelation as an effective-sample-size warning; availability of MacroIR) could be foregrounded more aggressively.
- **Empirical illustration**. The work is almost entirely simulation. A short demonstration on a published macroscopic recording (or even a re-analysis of a classic data set) would strengthen the claim that an experimenter can “fit their own recording.” The residual-whiteness check is usable on real data; showing it in action would help.
- **Presentation of uncertainty**. Some intermediate rungs (MR, VR) are retained mainly to dissect algebraic steps; their poorer calibration is interesting but can obscure the main message that IR is the first method whose reported intervals are demonstrably trustworthy across a wide domain.

None of these are fatal. The methodological gap is real, the diagnostic is classical and correctly applied, the simulation design is thorough, and the practical boundary (no overlapping regime in which fluctuations still inform amplitudes *and* least-squares error bars are honest) is a sharp, falsifiable claim.

### Fit to eLife
eLife has published both pure methods papers and rigorous re-examinations of statistical practice in the life sciences. This manuscript sits comfortably in that category: it diagnoses a systematic distortion that affects a widely used class of experiments, supplies a calibrated remedy, and releases software. The transparent use of exact simulation as ground truth, the explicit information-matrix diagnostics, and the mapping of failure regimes all align with eLife’s standards for reproducibility and honesty about limitations.

Minor revisions that would improve suitability:
- A shorter “for the experimentalist” summary of when to trust least squares versus when to switch to MacroIR, with the residual-autocorrelation diagnostic highlighted.
- Explicit discussion of how the two-state results are expected to degrade for multi-state schemes.
- One real-data vignette if feasible.

Overall assessment: **publishable in eLife after moderate revision focused on accessibility and scope statements**. The scientific core is solid, original in its diagnostic thoroughness, and of genuine utility to the ion-channel community.
