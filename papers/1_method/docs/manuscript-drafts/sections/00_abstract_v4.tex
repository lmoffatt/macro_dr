\begin{abstract}
Simple ion channel mechanisms leave a trail in the signal; complex ones need a calibrated likelihood. The flip state of P2X$_2$ showed up as a delay before the macroscopic current rose, with no model needed to see it. The asymmetric activation reported from the same recordings two decades later leaves none: it rests on ratios of evidence, and dropping the recursion from the likelihood put the top-ranked non-conformational alternative first. Where likelihood approximations break, and by how much, was never measured. The diagnostics are then classical: for a correctly specified likelihood the score, the log-likelihood's gradient, averages to zero at the true parameters and its covariance equals the Fisher information reported. We tested eight likelihoods, from standard least squares on the mean current, which leaves the correlated gating fluctuation unmodelled, to a filter conditioning each interval average on both ends. The sweep runs over channel number, noise and interval in a two-state scheme, ten thousand exact simulations of the process they approximate at every point measured. Those that leave the correlation unmodelled misreport their information by an order of magnitude wherever the residual retains enough memory, and no rescaling repairs it, because the distortion has directions in parameter space. The filter conditioning on both ends is calibrated over almost the whole plane, with departures growing toward few channels and low noise. No region of the measured plane gives both information about the unitary current and a trustworthy least-squares interval, the two boundaries one to three decades of noise apart.
\end{abstract}
