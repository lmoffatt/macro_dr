\begin{abstract}
Macroscopic ion-channel recordings carry stochastic fluctuations that the usual least-squares analysis discards. Likelihoods that use them have been available for two decades, including Bayesian recursive filters, and are rarely applied. Two reasons are plausible: unlike a least-squares fit, which can be judged from its residuals, a recursive filter tracks the data whether or not the uncertainty it reports is right; and they are hard to implement. We address both.
% TODO-SIGN: direction convention as fixed in Diagnostics; if the pending code read reverses it, flip every TODO-SIGN site in one pass.
At the true parameters a correct likelihood's score averages to zero and the covariance of that score equals the Fisher information it reports; the two departures measure bias and distortion. We measure both for a minimal kinetic scheme, swept over channel number, instrumental noise and acquisition interval, against data simulated exactly from the process it approximates.
Only the likelihood conditioned on both ends of the acquisition interval reports honestly across the plane, departing in a corner of few channels and low noise where the Gaussian closure itself fails. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/d4_distortion_envelopes.py over 1c2ae6f + 87889e6 + 0ffbda7 (1977 of 2100 points within 15%; the 123 departures sit at N_ch <= 500 and noise <= 0.5)
The intermediate members have no domain of their own, being calibrated only where least squares already is; and least squares is honest, and cheaper, exactly where instrumental noise exceeds gating noise, below a signal-to-noise ratio of the square root of the channel number. % src: domain scan over the same three directories (R calibrated where IR is not at 3 of 812 shared points, MR 2 of 336, NR 0 of 728, NMR 0 of 336, and all five at N_ch 10-20 where IR is conservative; R calibrated at 62 points against 134 failing at noise 0.1, and overwhelmingly calibrated above noise 1000) ; projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (LSE distortion crossing fitted at slope 1.01 and 1.03 in log noise-label against log N_ch; the label is a noise power, so the amplitude SNR at that boundary goes as sqrt(N_ch), which is the signal-to-gating-noise ratio N i p / (i sqrt(N p (1-p))) at p = 0.5)
R and Python packages return it with its score and Fisher information, so the criteria travel to any recording. % src: sections/07_backmatter.tex (macroir: R and Python bindings over one self-contained C++ core carrying the likelihood, its score and its Gaussian Fisher information)
\end{abstract}

% Kinetic schemes are usually fitted to macroscopic ion-channel currents by least squares on the deterministic mean, which discards the fluctuations. Likelihoods that use the fluctuations exist, including Bayesian recursive filters, and have almost no uptake, because whether such a filter reports its own uncertainty correctly was never characterised. Here we test a ladder of macroscopic likelihoods, from least squares to a filter conditioned on both ends of the acquisition interval, against data simulated exactly from the process they approximate. At the true parameters the score must average to zero and its covariance must equal the Fisher information reported; the mismatch is measurable. A likelihood that models the gating variance but carries no recursion is calibrated interval by interval and under-reports parameter uncertainty ten to fifteen fold once a record accumulates; a sandwich correction restores nominal coverage.
