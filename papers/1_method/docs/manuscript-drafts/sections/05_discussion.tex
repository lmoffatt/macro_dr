A usable calibrated likelihood for most experimental conditions on macrocurrents is demonstrated here for two states: the boundary-conditioned member we published with the P2X$_2$ analysis \citep{moffatt2025bayesian}. Over most of the design space it reports an uncertainty close to the one it delivers, and where it does not, the departure is measured rather than assumed. The reported interval is mildly the wrong width in the few-channel, low-noise corner, and there the repair is the sandwich the diagnostic already supplies (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}--figure supplement 4).

A simple statistic tells an experimenter which regime a recording is in: the integrated autocorrelation time of its standardized least-squares residual, read off a fit already being made, with no ensemble and no known truth. As the instrumental noise grows the recorded current becomes Gaussian whatever the member assumes, and the window-averaged least-squares fit recovers a calibrated error bar there, its closing-rate distortion falling from thirteen to one across five decades of noise (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}). The refuge has a price: the noise at which that error bar becomes calibrated lies one to three decades above the noise at which the fluctuations stop carrying the unitary current, so no measured region gives both (Figure~\ref{fig:usage}). The reading is necessary and not sufficient: memory implies distortion, and whiteness on its own certifies nothing (Figure~\ref{fig:memory}).

The acquisition average is the macroscopic form of the missed-event problem. A single-channel record loses the transitions that fall inside its dead time \citep{colquhoun1995fitting}; a macroscopic record loses the ones that fall inside its sampling window, where the excursions of every channel are averaged together before anything is recorded. What is recorded, in both places, is not the state of the process at any one time.

At first we expected the one-endpoint member to be a fair approximation: a mean conductance plus a noise of its own, the emission form \citet{munch2022bayesian} give to open-channel noise, with that noise computed from the averaging. It is not. It shaves the amplitude bias and loses the second moment, ending worse on the parameter it was built for than the member that ignores the averaging altogether, $1.16$ against $1.01$ at ten thousand channels. We tried the variance alone next, keeping the one-endpoint update and taking the interval variance from the two-endpoint construction, in case a mis-stated variance was the whole of it. It buys back more of the amplitude bias and loses the second moment in the same way, and neither partial correction reaches either interval member (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}--figure supplement 3).

The Markov property came to the rescue. Conditioning on both endpoints is what makes the interval average a function of the states again, and it is also what leaves one state variable per junction, so the same choice recovers the average and keeps the chain Markov across the record (Appendix~\ref{app:derivation}). The integration and the recursion are bought together, and what comes out is a white score, the innovation whiteness that has certified an optimal filter since \citet{mehra1970identification}.

What is left over factors into two parts that say different things, a per-sample failure and a failure across intervals, and the recursion reaches the second: the local time correlation \citet{milescu2005maximum} named, now measured (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}--figure supplement 1).

\subsection*{The channel number stays in the model and stops informing the record}

Two parameters go quiet once the agonist is removed, and for different reasons. The opening rate leaves the generator with the ligand, and reaches the record afterwards only through the occupancy the pulse left behind, which is why it takes a few samples to fall silent rather than none. The channel number stays in the generator and is cancelled anyway: a member conditioned on the past already carries the number of channels still open, so a parameter that reaches the record only through that number has nothing left to give, and its information falls to the numerical floor within six intervals (Figure~\ref{fig:time}--figure supplements 1 and 2). The members that do not condition go on claiming a sixth of their pulse total about the channel count across the washout. A short pulse therefore buys the closing rate and the unitary current, and not the other two. The numbers are from one design cell.

\subsection*{Grouping samples is the same measurement at a larger window}

samples is the same measurement at a larger $\Delta$, with a proportionately smaller instrumental
variance and no sample discarded (Results), so the temporal structure of the record becomes

hardly at all for an amplitude one; after a concentration change the groups can grow geometrically
with the transient they follow, which is legitimate rather than an approximation because the
likelihood takes the window as an argument and not as a constant; and the cost of the fit follows
the design, since the recursion is linear in the groups it is given.
