A mechanism can leave a mark no model is needed to see: the flip state of P2X2 showed up as a delay before the current rises. Yet kinetic schemes are usually fitted by least squares to the mean current, discarding the gating fluctuations. Recursive likelihoods that use them have been available for two decades and are little used: a filter tracks the data even when the uncertainty it reports is wrong, and where such approximations break was never located, for want of a known truth. The instruments are then classical: at the true parameters the score, the log-likelihood's gradient, averages to zero, and its covariance across recordings equals the Fisher information reported. We tested eight likelihoods, from least squares to a filter conditioning each interval average on both ends, over channel number, noise and interval in a two-state scheme, ten thousand recordings per cell. Those that leave the correlation unmodelled misreport their information by an order of magnitude wherever the residual retains memory, and no single factor repairs it, because the distortion has directions. The boundary-conditioned likelihood is calibrated over almost the whole plane, departing toward few channels and low noise, where the Gaussian moment closure on the occupancy is misspecified. No region of the measured plane gives both information about the unitary current and a trustworthy least-squares interval, the two boundaries one to three decades of noise apart. An experimenter can fit their own recording and test whether the reported interval can be trusted.
