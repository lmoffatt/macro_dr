A recursive likelihood cannot be checked against a record by eye. Least squares predicts each
sample from the parameters alone, so the prediction can be laid over the data and judged where it
misses. A filter predicts each sample from the samples before it, so it follows the record whether
or not the uncertainty it reports is the uncertainty it delivers. What follows measures the second
against the first, on recordings simulated exactly from the process every member approximates.

Throughout, $N_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the number of channels in the patch, $k_{\mathrm{on}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{off}}$ the opening and closing rates, $i$ the unitary current, $\Delta$ the acquisition interval and $\tau = 1/k_{\mathrm{off}}$ the closing time constant, so that $\Delta\!\cdot\!k_{\mathrm{off}}$ is the interval in units of $\tau$. The instrumental noise is reported as the label the runs and the figure axes carry; the dimensionless noise $\tilde S$, the instrumental noise power accumulated over one channel time constant measured against the unitary current, is one tenth of that label (Methods).

\subsection*{One filter step along the cost ladder}

Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} puts one turn of the filter cycle for six members side by side, on
a single simulated recording of a patch of $N_{\mathrm{ch}}=20$ channels. It fixes the vocabulary and
carries no measured claim.

Least squares (\texttt{LSE}) fits the deterministic mean current and assigns one constant variance
$\hat\sigma^2$ to every residual, estimated as the residual sum of squares over the record, so its
predicted band has the same width whether the channels are gating or not. The non-recursive member
(\texttt{NR}) replaces that constant with the per-interval gating variance the model predicts, so its
band follows the open population, while its update row stays an open loop: the occupancy carried into
each interval comes from the initial condition and never from the data. The recursive member
(\texttt{R}) adds the Bayesian update, on a conductance taken at a single instant. The
boundary-conditioned member (\texttt{IR}) conditions the interval-averaged conductance on the channel
states at both ends of the interval, which lets the covariance between them enter the gain. Each
column adds one operation to the one before it, and the cost of a likelihood evaluation rises with it.

Figure~\ref{fig:clouds} turns the ladder into a statement about reported uncertainty at one cell:
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}=100$, noise label $0.1$ ($\tilde S = 0.01$), $\Delta\!\cdot\!k_{\mathrm{off}} = 0.1$.
Ten thousand independent recordings are partitioned into groups of ten, each group is fitted by
maximum likelihood under each member, and the scatter of the resulting thousand estimates is compared
against the covariance that member reports from its own Gaussian Fisher information.

The two objects disagree by an order of magnitude at the foot of the ladder and by nothing at its
top. Taking the ratio of the area of the empirical $95\%$ ellipse to the area of the reported one,
and averaging geometrically over the six pairs the four informative parameters form, the open-loop
member reads $11.41$, the recursive instantaneous member $1.17$ and the boundary-conditioned member
$1.01$; on the joint four-parameter ellipse the same three read $154.65$, $1.56$ and $1.12$. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_2.Rmd (diagnostics chunk, empirical over Fisher; per-pair ranges are in the caption)
A likelihood that carries no recursion under-reports the width of the parameter distribution by a
factor of ten to fifteen, and the recursive members are wrong by tens of percent.

The fall between them is not monotone, and this is where the ladder stops being an ordering. On the
kinetic pair the recursive instantaneous filter reads $1.32$ while \texttt{MR} reads $1.97$ and
\texttt{VR} reads $2.19$, and only then does \texttt{IR} return to $1.02$; the amplitude and noise
pairs run the same way. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_2.Rmd (diagnostics chunk; amplitude pair 1.09/1.53/1.77/1.00, noise pair 1.06/1.29/1.43/0.99)
Conditioning the conductance on one endpoint costs calibration rather than buying it, in every pair
and in both directions of the variance switch, and what recovers it is the second endpoint entering
the gain (Appendix~1). The two intermediate rungs are kept in the figure for that reason: they are
the step from \texttt{R} to \texttt{IR} taken in its two algebraic halves, and the half that changes
only the interval variance moves the answer the wrong way.

What the acquisition average buys on its own is separable here, because one member isolates it. The
one-endpoint non-recursive member conditions the conductance on the interval instead of on an instant
and adds nothing else, and against the open-loop member it improves every pair, most on the noise
pair, from $8.84$ to $3.28$. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_2.Rmd (diagnostics chunk; kinetic 14.83 to 12.59, amplitude 10.04 to 9.00, noise 8.84 to 3.28)
The reason is in the first moment. The open-loop member carries no interval-mean conductance
variance, so the gating variance it predicts is too small, and the fit makes up the difference by
inflating the one free parameter that can absorb it: its noise estimate lands a factor of $2.2$ above
the truth, against every other member within $0.04$ in $\log_{10}$. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_2.Rmd (noise row; NR log10 sigma -3.67 against a simulated -4)
Restoring that term returns the noise estimate to within $0.005$ in $\log_{10}$. Modelling the
acquisition average therefore buys the noise level, and it buys it with no recursion at all.

The distortion is measurable and it is also removable. Replacing each reported covariance by the
sandwich returns every gating-aware member of the ladder to within about a tenth of one, with every
parameter pair between $0.87$ and $1.07$, and it is least squares alone that it leaves short at
$1.63$. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/D-4_ranking_verdict.md §1a (emp/corrected column) ; figure_2.Rmd diagnostics chunk (NR 1.03, R 0.95, IR 1.05; corrected LSE 1.63 against 0.96-1.07 for the other six)
The reader-facing form of that repair is coverage. Reducing the estimates to their squared
Mahalanobis distance from the cloud mean, which is $\chi^2$ with as many degrees of freedom as free
parameters if the reported covariance is right, the nominal $95\%$ region of \texttt{IR} covers
$0.914$ of them under its own Fisher covariance and $0.947$ to $0.949$ under the sandwich, at
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}=10$ and noise label $0.05$ over a thousand fits in six parameters at group size
$100$ (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}--figure supplement 2). That one correction repairs six of the seven
rungs, without being told which of them is wrong or how, is what makes the distortion a usable
quantity rather than a complaint.

Least squares carries a column in the kinetic pair and none in the other two, for reasons of
identifiability rather than coverage: its grid configuration holds the unitary current and the noise
level fixed at the simulated values (Methods), so both ellipses would have zero area and the ratio
would be $0/0$. The mean current depends on the channel number and the unitary current only through
their product, and it is the gating fluctuations, whose variance carries a second power of the
unitary current, that separate the two.

The miscalibration of Figure~\ref{fig:clouds} is a property of the likelihood and not of the
optimiser, and Figure~\ref{fig:time} shows where in the recording it is produced. One common set of a
thousand recordings at the same cell is scored interval by interval by each member, with no fitting
involved. The least-squares arm here is the six-parameter configuration, which keeps the unitary
current and the noise level free so that its per-interval quantities align index for index with the
macroscopic dumps (Methods); nothing measured in this subsection may be carried across to the
four-parameter configuration of the later figures.

The members separate first on the log-likelihood they assign to the same data, over a span of $118.9$
nats from the bottom of the ladder to the top, and most of that span is bought by modelling the
gating fluctuations at all. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3.html (caption-numbers chunk of figure_3.Rmd; ensemble-mean total -262 +- 0.73 for LSE and -143 +- 0.24 for IR over 1000 recordings)

The hinge of the argument is that per interval every member reports its information correctly. The
variance of the per-interval score matches the per-interval Fisher information for all four, with the
median of $\log_{10}(J_t/F_t)$ over the informative steps inside $\pm0.19$ of zero for every member
and every parameter. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3_supplement_1.html (caption-numbers chunk, D-G table; k_on +0.184 LSE, -0.038 NR, -0.181 R, +0.006 IR; k_off -0.145 R, +0.033 IR. Re-measured from the digests 2026-07-31 and reproducing the archived notebook to five decimals)
Accumulated over the recording the same ratio reads $14.96$ for \texttt{LSE} and $13.63$ for
\texttt{NR}, against $1.07$ and $1.08$ for the two recursive members. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3.html (caption-numbers chunk; accumulated J_T/F_T with bootstrap intervals 13.86-15.93, 12.52-14.95, 0.975-1.17, 0.995-1.17)
The two non-recursive members are within a few percent of the identity at each interval and off by
more than an order of magnitude once the intervals are summed. The open-loop member also changes the
sign of the discrepancy between the per-step and the accumulated statistic, and a per-sample
modelling error cannot do that.

The least-squares ratio needs the qualification the Diagnostics section attaches to it. The Gaussian
Fisher information of a least-squares fit presumes one constant residual variance, and the exact
simulator violates that premise by construction, because the variance of a recorded interval rises
and falls with the open population. The factor of fifteen is the size of that break, and it is not an
information-matrix-equality failure of the same kind as the open-loop gating likelihood's, where the
predictive variance does follow the gating. The two ratios are reported side by side and are not read
as two values of one comparable quantity.

The mechanism is visible in the same figure. At lag one the autocorrelation of the per-interval score
on $k_{\mathrm{off}}$ is $-0.0043 \pm 0.0043$ for \texttt{IR}, indistinguishable from zero, against
$0.191 \pm 0.004$ for \texttt{R}, $0.864 \pm 0.0019$ for \texttt{NR} and $0.856 \pm 0.0022$ for
\texttt{LSE}. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3.html (caption-numbers chunk; lag-one score ACF, k_off)
A positively correlated score makes the variance of its sum exceed the sum of the per-interval
informations, which is how the per-interval identity holds while the accumulated one fails. The
temporal correlation the two non-recursive members leave in their score is the local time correlation
of the current that \citet{milescu2005maximum} named as the dominant error of independent-interval
fitting, measured here at the level of the inference rather than the data.

Row C of Figure~\ref{fig:time} carries a second result. For the members that condition on the past,
the information about the channel number and the opening rate reaches the numerical floor within six
intervals of the agonist being removed: having conditioned on everything up to that point, the filter
gains nothing further from watching the channels decay. Two parameters survive the washout by
different routes. The closing rate survives through the mean, which is the shape of the decay its own
kinetics govern, and the unitary current through the variance, which is the gating fluctuation of the
channels still open. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3_supplement_1.html (washout table, row C: mean F_t over the last ten intervals for IR, k_on 7.9e-44, N_ch 1.4e-21, unitary current 0.66, k_off 1.13; 81% of what k_off keeps is from the mean and 71% of what i keeps is from the variance)
This is a property of conditioning rather than of the macroscopic observable: the two non-recursive
members retain $15$ to $16\%$ of their pulse information about the channel number through the
washout, against $0.5$ to $0.8\%$ for the two recursive ones. % src: same table, columns by algorithm
The parameters the figure has no colours for, and the first moment of the residual, are in
Figure~\ref{fig:time}--figure supplement 3; the instrumental noise level is the one parameter
measured before the agonist arrives, and its score is white even in the members whose kinetic
directions are not, so the memory that carries the accumulated error is a property of the gating
directions. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3_supplement_3.html (caption-numbers chunk: noise informative at 100 of 100 intervals with 20 pre-agonist against 0 for k_on and i; lag-one noise score ACF -0.004 NR, -0.008 R, both covering zero)

One cell is not a result, and the plane carries one the cell cannot: instrumental noise equalises the
members. Where it grows past the gating variance every rung reports honestly, so the differences
between them are a property of the regime in which the fluctuations still carry information, and not
of the algorithms in general. Everything below is read against that.

Figure~\ref{fig:plane} repeats the two moments over the whole grid, with the members across the
columns, the channel count down the rows, and the acquisition interval against the instrumental noise
inside each panel. It splits the display by moment rather than by member, so the least-squares arm
stays alongside the likelihoods it is being compared with. Half (A) is evaluated at the simulation
truth, where a bias is visible; half (B) at the pooled optimum, where the score vanishes by
construction and the information comparison is clean (Methods).

More channels do not repair the recursive instantaneous filter. Taking the median over the seven
intervals at noise label $0.1$, its $k_{\mathrm{off}}$ distortion sits on a flat floor near $1.4$
across four decades of channel count while the boundary-conditioned member converges to one over the
same range; the open-loop member moves the other way, from $22$ to $77$, and the least-squares arm
holds a floor near $13$. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4.Rmd (caption-numbers chunk; R 1.372/1.440/1.439/1.42, IR 1.324/1.099/1.000/1.00, NR 21.92/16.83/31.32/76.52, LSE 11.79/13.06/13.04/12.97 across N_ch 10 to 10^4)

The first moment carries a separate cost. The recursive instantaneous filter has a bias in the
channel number of about $0.14$ in $\log_{10}$ units, close to a $35$ to $40\%$ error that does not
shrink as channels are added; the open-loop member carries half of that in the same direction, and
the boundary-conditioned member is at or near zero everywhere. Least squares has a rate bias of
essentially zero over the same plane, and that concession is worth making plainly: the classical fit
recovers the rates, and what it gets wrong is how well it says it recovered them. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4.Rmd (caption-numbers chunk; R 0.126/0.148/0.138/0.138, NR 0.073-0.084)

Read together, the two moments say that the two axes of the family act on different failures and that
neither substitutes for the other. Averaging over the acquisition interval is what removes the
amplitude bias: the median absolute bias in the channel number is $0.099$ and $0.110$ in $\log_{10}$
units for the two members that do not average, against $0.003$ and $0.002$ for their interval-averaged
counterparts, so recursion alone does not remove it and slightly worsens it. Recursion is what removes
the distortion: the same step drops the closing-rate distortion from tens to about $1.4$, and it
reaches one when the recursion conditions on both ends of the interval. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4.Rmd (caption-numbers chunk; median |bias| in N_ch by member)

Figure~\ref{fig:time}: interval averaging alone leaves the per-interval residual calibrated and the
accumulated information ratio at $20.8$, recursion alone inverts that pair to $0.782$ and $1.07$, and

A member that takes one axis and not the other repairs one failure and keeps the other, which is why
the family cannot be ordered on a single scale. Nothing in this statement is specific to ion
channels: it holds for any state model whose observations are averages over a window.

The two recursive members also differ in where on the plane their departures sit. Counting the
fraction of cells that depart from one by at least $15\%$, parameter by parameter, the recursive
instantaneous member departs on between a fifth and a half of the plane in four of the five
directions, against at most a twelfth for the boundary-conditioned one. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4.Rmd (caption-numbers chunk; R 0.4375/0.5625/0.2232/0.3125/0.0000 and IR 0.0179/0.0804/0.0000/0.0268/0.0000 for k_on, k_off, i, N_ch and the noise level)
The distortion of the instantaneous filter is spread across parameters and across the plane; the
distortion of the boundary-conditioned filter is confined to the few-channel corner. Conditioning
localises the error as well as shrinking it.

Instrumental noise moves the whole picture continuously, and it moves the window-ignoring members
furthest. The closing-rate distortion of least squares falls from its floor near $13$ to one over
about three decades of added noise, at every channel count, and the recursive instantaneous filter
follows the same pattern about three decades lower. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4.Rmd (caption-numbers chunk; at N_ch 10, LSE 11.79 at label 0.1, 1.14 at 100, 1.00 at 1000; at N_ch 10^4, 12.97, then 2.54 at 10^4, 1.15 at 10^5, 1.00 at 10^6; R 1.42, 1.03 at 100, 1.00 at 1000 at N_ch 10^4)
The reason is that instrumental noise adds a variance that carries no gating memory, so as it grows
it dilutes the temporal structure a window-ignoring likelihood fails to model, until there is nothing
left to fail on. The channel count at which each member's error bar becomes honest therefore rises
with the noise level, which is the crossover Figure~\ref{fig:usage} draws as a boundary.

Over the whole measured grid, counting every parameter at every cell, the reported uncertainty is
within $\pm15\%$ of the delivered one for $93\%$ of the boundary-conditioned member's points, $70\%$
of the recursive instantaneous member's and $31\%$ of the open-loop member's. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4.Rmd (2116 of 2268 over 560 cells; 684 of 980 over 238 cells; 277 of 896 over 224 cells; the one-endpoint member carried in the supplement reaches 20%, 67 of 336)
Least squares carries no entry in this comparison, because its distortion matrix has four dimensions
against the likelihood members' six and the two counts are not commensurable (Methods). The coverage
is unequal and has to be quoted with the number: the boundary-conditioned member spans eleven channel
counts from $5$ to $10^4$ across the twelve noise levels of the ladder (Methods), not on a full
rectangle, and the others cover less.

The mechanism behind the flat floor is the split of the distortion into a per-sample part and a
correlation part (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}--figure supplement 1). On the closing rate at noise label
$0.1$, the per-sample part goes to one as channels are added for both recursive members, $1.09$ to
$0.998$ for \texttt{R} and $1.10$ to $1.007$ for \texttt{IR} across the four decades, so the
single-interval Gaussian approximation becomes exact for both at the same rate. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4_S1.Rmd, caption-numbers chunk (sample-part medians, k_off at noise 0.1). Re-knitted 2026-08-05 on figures/data/{1c2ae6f,87889e6,0ffbda7}: R 1.086 -> 0.998, IR 1.099 -> 1.007, both reproduced to the last digit
What separates them is the correlation part, which rises for \texttt{R} from $1.35$ to $1.47$ and
falls for \texttt{IR} from $1.24$ to $1.00$. % src: same chunk, correlation-part medians; re-knitted 2026-08-05, both reproduced exactly
The two members are equally faithful to a single interval, and they part company on how much temporal
dependence they leave in the score, which is what conditioning on both interval endpoints removes.

Two features of the plane are easy to misread. Where the acquisition interval is far shorter than the
channel's correlation time, successive samples carry redundant information, and a faithful likelihood
has to register that redundancy, so a large correlation distortion at the shortest intervals is the
correct cost of oversampling rather than a defect. And at $N_{\mathrm{ch}}=10^4$ with
$\Delta\!\cdot\!k_{\mathrm{off}}=1$ the amplitude directions of the recursive instantaneous filter are
not identified: the Gaussian Fisher information is ill-conditioned there, so a displacement that costs
almost no likelihood is amplified into a large parameter shift. Those cells are drawn grey. Their
sign and their existence are real; their magnitude is a property of the conditioning.

A reader who accepts the distortion will reach for the cheapest repair before changing likelihood. If
a likelihood over-states its information, declare that the recording holds fewer independent
intervals than it appears to and deflate the count. The number that repair would use is already
measured: the integrated autocorrelation of the standardized residual, which counts how many
intervals a likelihood effectively has for every interval it believes it has, reads $1.008$ for
\texttt{IR}, $1.30$ for \texttt{R}, $3.93$ for \texttt{NR} and $9.26$ for \texttt{LSE} at noise label
$0.1$. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4_supplement_lag_kappa.Rmd (integrated residual autocorrelation over the plane; values at noise label 0.1)
The classical fit therefore behaves as though it had about nine times more independent observations
than the recording supplies, which is the degrees-of-freedom cost of fitting correlated data as
though it were independent, and it is a quantity an experimentalist can compute on a real recording
without an ensemble and without a known truth.

The repair works if the distortion is a scalar, every parameter direction wrong by the same factor.
Figure~\ref{fig:plane}--figure supplement 3 measures whether it is, by splitting the distortion matrix
into the two invariants of its spectrum: the geometric mean of the eigenvalues, which is the typical
factor by which the reported information is wrong over all directions at once, and the spread of
those eigenvalues about it, which is how much that factor varies from direction to direction. The two
are the orthogonal components of the affine-invariant distance the diagnostic already reports, so a
spread of one means a single rescaling is exact and the factor it should use is the magnitude.

The spread is not one where it would matter. The least-squares arm and the open-loop interval member
each over-state their information by a factor of about $5$ to $6$, and the per-direction factor
spreads by about $3.2$ around it, flat across four decades of channel count, so directions exist that
are wrong by twenty and directions exist that are barely wrong at all. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_4_magnitude_anisotropy.Rmd (caption-numbers chunk, medians over the seven intervals at noise 0.1, pool anchor: magnitude ILSE 5.79/6.10/6.15/6.08 and INR 5.16/5.01/4.94/4.93; anisotropy ILSE 3.17/3.20/3.17/3.22 and INR 3.18/3.26/3.28/3.30 across N_ch 10 to 10^4)
No single effective count serves both. The claim a reader would otherwise make, that the residual
autocorrelation supplies the correction, is refuted by the same measurement that would have supplied
the number, and the two boundaries of the map below are therefore boundaries on which likelihood to
use and not on which correction to apply.

The three recursive members separate on different axes of the same pair. The recursive instantaneous
member's magnitude drifts up with the channel count while its spread sits flat at about $1.18$, so
its failure is mostly a size it could in principle correct for. The boundary-conditioned member is at
one in magnitude at every count and its spread falls toward one, so it is the member converging on
both axes at once; at ten channels its spread is $1.25$, above the strict criterion, which is the
same few-channel corner every other measurement in this section points at. % src: same chunk (magnitude R 1.09-1.24, MR 1.30-1.56, IR 1.02-1.00; anisotropy R 1.29-1.19, MR 1.40-1.39, IR 1.25-1.04)
The one-endpoint member is worse than the member it improves on, on both axes and at every channel
count, which is the ladder claim in its sharpest form: a partial interval correction does not
interpolate between the member without one and the member with a complete one.

\subsection*{Where the calibrated member departs, and which moment carries it}

Figure~\ref{fig:limit} resolves the corner where the boundary-conditioned member departs, on a scale
that Figure~\ref{fig:plane} cannot show: there a factor of $1.5$ is a faint tint, and here it needs an
axis. Finding that corner was half the object of the measurement, since a diagnostic that never fires
on the member it is applied to says nothing about what it can resolve.

The departure is two-sided within a single cell. At ten channels and $\tilde S = 0.005$ the reported
error bar of the closing rate is too small by a factor of $1.62$ at the shortest interval, while that
of the channel number is too large by a factor of $0.65$, so one likelihood is over-confident about a
rate and conservative about a count at the same time and in the same recording. % src: as the figure
The two parts of the decomposition answer to the acquisition interval in opposite directions. Across
that same cell the correlation part of the closing rate falls from $1.52$ to $1.27$ as the interval
coarsens, while the per-sample part rises from $1.06$ to $1.19$: sampling more coarsely buys back the
memory and pays for it in the per-sample non-Gaussianity. Both parts vanish by $\tilde S = 1$, where
the instrumental noise has made the emission Gaussian enough that neither closure is strained. % src: as the figure

Two limits travel with the figure. The family applies both of its Gaussian approximations at every
step and the score sees only their product, so a departure measured here cannot be assigned to one of
them; separating them needs the microscopic recursion, which is a companion paper's subject. And the
grid is truncated where the effect grows: the departure of the closing rate is still accelerating at
the lowest simulated noise, reading $1.22$, $1.37$ and $1.52$ over the last three noise steps at ten
channels, so the figure locates the onset and not the far side. % src: as the figure

Figure~\ref{fig:usage} collects the measurements into one plane, the channel number against the
instrumental noise, at a single acquisition interval $\Delta\!\cdot\!k_{\mathrm{off}}=0.1$. Its
content is the boundaries: the measured cells are the input and the lines are the output. Four are
drawn, and they are two questions asked of two methods. Two ask whether a method's reported error bar
is honest, one for the boundary-conditioned likelihood and one for least squares, and are drawn where
that method's information distortion crosses $1.15$, a $7\%$ error on the reported standard
deviation. Two ask whether a parameter is reachable at all, and are drawn where the
distortion-corrected standard error crosses a factor of two.

Read upward at a fixed channel count, the recording loses capabilities in a fixed order, which gives
five regions, named and read in order in the caption. Below the lowest boundary the Gaussian closure
is itself misspecified and the recording is not a macroscopic problem. Above it the likelihood
delivers the unitary current, the channel number and an honest interval while least squares reports
an interval several times too narrow; then the amplitude pair stops being separable and the rates
alone survive; then least squares is calibrated too and buys the same answer more cheaply; and above
that nothing useful is left. The lowest boundary is the one with a negative slope, measured at $-0.56$
in $\mathrm{d}\log_{10}\mathrm{noise}/\mathrm{d}\log_{10}N_{\mathrm{ch}}$, because instrumental noise
makes the emission more Gaussian and so relaxes the occupancy closure while hurting everything else. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (fitted slopes)

The boundary at which the classical error bar becomes honest is the one this paper predicts and then
measures. Fitted in logarithms over four measured crossings in each of two parameter directions, it
runs as instrumental noise proportional to channel number, with slopes of $1.01$ and $1.03$. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (crossings at noise label 164 and 130 at N_ch 10, 1.28e3 and 1.53e3 at 100, 1.54e4 and 1.51e4 at 1000, 1.62e5 and 1.59e5 at 10^4, channel-number and closing-rate directions)
That is the crossover the mechanism predicts, since the gating variance grows with the channel count
while the instrumental variance does not, so the ratio of the two decides whether a window-ignoring
fit is honest. Here it is a measured slope rather than an assumed scaling.

The ordering of the boundaries is the result the map exists to state. There is no region of the
measured plane in which the amplitude still carries information and the classical error bar is
simultaneously trustworthy; the two boundaries are separated by one to three decades of noise
everywhere measured. Whenever the fluctuations still say something about the unitary current or the
channel number, a classical fit reports a confidence interval that is too narrow. The scope of that
statement has to be stated with it: the classical arm here is least squares on the deterministic mean
current with the unitary current held fixed, in its four-parameter configuration. It is not the
variance-mean regression, this paper does not benchmark non-stationary fluctuation analysis, and
nothing here should be read as a measured comparison against it.

The map also fixes where a parameter stops being reachable at any noise level. Minimising the
corrected standard error over the whole noise sweep, the channel number is unreachable below about
$17$ channels and the opening rate below about $52$, at this acquisition interval. The unitary current
and the closing rate have no such floor within the simulated range. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (reachability floors)

The evidence is thinner than the picture, and the subsection says so rather than leaving it to a
caption. The two boundaries at which least squares becomes honest, and the closure floor, are each
fitted through four measured crossings. The channel count at which the third region opens spans $120$
to $1368$ depending only on whether the strict or the loose criterion is used. The lower vertex is
extrapolated to $N_{\mathrm{ch}}$ between $3$ and $9$, below the simulated floor of ten channels, and
