A recursive likelihood cannot be checked against a record by eye: it predicts each sample from the
samples before it, so it follows the record whether or not the uncertainty it reports is the
uncertainty it delivers. What follows measures it against least squares, on recordings simulated
exactly from the process every member approximates.

Throughout, $k_{\mathrm{on}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{off}}$ are the opening and closing rates, $i$ the unitary current and $\tau = 1/k_{\mathrm{off}}$ the closing time constant, and a tilde marks the natural units of the model: $\widetilde{\Delta} = \Delta\,k_{\mathrm{off}} = \Delta/\tau$ is the acquisition interval, and $\widetilde{S}$ the instrumental noise power accumulated over one channel time constant against the single-channel signal power (Methods). The two least-squares arms are one flag apart, \texttt{LSE} fitting the conductance at the sample and \texttt{ILSE} fitting it averaged over the acquisition window, and both are measured. \subsection*{Recovery at one design cell}

Figure~\ref{fig:clouds} turns the ladder into a statement about reported uncertainty at one cell,
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}=100$, $\widetilde{S} = 0.01$, $\widetilde{\Delta} = 0.1$.

The interval average is what centres the estimate, and the roster isolates that step twice, one flag
apart in each family: \texttt{LSE} against \texttt{ILSE}, and \texttt{NR} against \texttt{INR}. In
the kinetic pair every member lands within $0.04$ in $\log_{10}$ of the truth, and both times the
interval average takes a factor of two to three off the offset in $k_{\mathrm{on}}$, while recursion
on its own does the opposite: the recursive instantaneous filter is the least centred member of the
pair at $-0.040$, worse than the open-loop member it is built from, and only \texttt{IR}, which has
both, has no offset resolved from zero in either coordinate. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_first_moment.R (per-parameter table; 1000 estimates, 2000-resample percentile bootstrap, type 6. k_on: LSE -0.0148, ILSE -0.0069, NR -0.0210, INR -0.0077, R -0.0401, MR -0.0310, VR -0.0021 unresolved, IR +0.0015 unresolved. k_off: MR -0.0090, VR -0.0123, NR +0.0104, the rest at or under 0.003)

In the amplitude and noise directions the same split costs an order of magnitude more, and there the
displacement has a mechanism the panel can be read for. The unitary current comes out low by between $0.103$ and
$0.228$ in $\log_{10}$ for \texttt{NR}, \texttt{VR}, \texttt{R} and \texttt{MR}, a factor of $0.59$ at
the worst, against $0.028$ for \texttt{INR} and nothing resolved for \texttt{IR}, and the channel
number comes out high by almost exactly the same amount, while every member recovers the product
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}\,i$ that the mean current determines to within $0.023$. % src: same script (amplitude-split table: bias in log10 i, in log10 N_ch and in their sum, per member). The per-member series was retired to a range on 2026-08-10 and is kept here: log10 i bias MR -0.228, R -0.156, VR -0.117, NR -0.103, INR -0.028.
What separates the two factors is the gating variance, which carries a second power of the unitary
current, so a member with the wrong interval variance splits a well-determined product wrongly. The
noise level takes the same shortfall: \texttt{NR} models no interval-mean conductance variance, and
the fit absorbs what is missing in the one free parameter that can take it, landing its noise
estimate $0.333$ in $\log_{10}$ above the truth, a factor of $2.2$, against every other member within
$0.04$. Conditioning on the interval instead of on an instant, and adding nothing else, returns it to
$0.005$.

The open marker in each panel is the truth displaced by the predicted bias, and that comparison is
partial. On $k_{\mathrm{off}}$ the prediction falls inside the sampling interval of the measured
displacement for all eight members; in the amplitude directions it carries the sign and $71$ to
$85\%$ of the size for \texttt{NR}, \texttt{R}, \texttt{MR} and \texttt{VR}, and it over-states
\texttt{NR}'s noise offset by a factor of $1.8$. % src: same script (predicted-against-measured table, with the bootstrap interval of the measured value)
The first moment is diagnosed to within a fifth rather than reproduced, which is what a first-order
prediction at the truth can do for a cloud fitted in groups of ten; the quantitative claim of this
section is the second moment.

Averaging is worth a great deal to a likelihood that also models the gating variance and next to
nothing to one that does not.

A size is not a diagnosis, because the distortion also has a shape. Resizing the reported ellipse to
the empirical area, the most any single factor applied to an error bar can do, does not land it on
the empirical one. The spread of the two distortion eigenvalues runs from $1.0$ to $3.2$ across the
panels, against a finite-sample floor of at least $1.08$, the $95$th percentile of the same statistic
when a thousand estimates in two dimensions are drawn from a perfectly calibrated covariance, and a
lower bound because the reported covariance is itself estimated. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_shape_and_floor.R (per-pair size, shape and eigendirections; the floor from 20,000 null draws, median 1.038 and 95th percentile 1.081 at p=2, n=1000)
In the worst panel, \texttt{INR} in the noise pair, the two numbers read $3.3$ and $3.2$ and factor
into a variance ratio of $10.5$ along one direction and $1.03$ along the other, and the calibrated
direction is the noise level itself, within a tenth of a degree of that axis. The member that
repairs the noise estimate reports the noise uncertainty exactly right and the channel-number
uncertainty with a standard error $3.2$ times too small, so the single factor a scalar correction
would apply, $3.3$, is right for neither. The alignment is not particular to that member: in the
same pair \texttt{MR} and \texttt{VR} read $1.04$ and $1.02$ along the noise axis against $1.59$ and
$2.02$ across it. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_shape_and_floor.R (per-pair eigendirections, noise pair)

The distortion is removable, provided the correction is a matrix. Replacing
each reported covariance by the sandwich returns every gating-aware member of the ladder to within
about a tenth of one, with every parameter pair between $0.87$ and $1.07$, and it is the
least-squares family alone that it leaves short, at $1.65$ and $1.63$ for the two arms. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/D-4_ranking_verdict.md §1a (emp/corrected column) ; papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_shape_and_floor.R (corrected column: LSE 1.65, ILSE 1.63, NR 1.07, INR 1.05, R 0.96, MR 0.93, VR 0.97, IR 1.01 in the kinetic pair)
The two arms fail it by the same amount while differing in every first-moment quantity, which puts
the failure in what they share and not in the interval average. In coverage terms, the nominal $95\%$
region of \texttt{IR} holds $0.914$ of the estimates under its own Fisher covariance and $0.947$ to
$0.949$ under the sandwich, over a thousand fits in six parameters at group size $100$, at
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}=10$ and $\widetilde{S} = 0.005$ (Figure~\ref{fig:plane}--figure supplement 2).

optimiser, and Figure~\ref{fig:time} shows where in the recording it is produced, scoring one common

is the six-parameter configuration, which keeps the unitary current and the noise level free so that
its per-interval quantities align index for index with the macroscopic dumps (Methods); nothing
measured in this subsection may be carried across to the four-parameter configuration of the later
figures.

The members separate first on the log-likelihood they assign to the same data, over a span of $118.9$
nats from the bottom of the ladder to the top, and most of that span is bought by modelling the
gating fluctuations at all. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3.html (caption-numbers chunk of figure_3.Rmd; ensemble-mean total -262 +- 0.73 for LSE and -143 +- 0.24 for IR over 1000 recordings)

log-likelihood to the same data, $-262$ nats, and agree to three significant figures on every
statistic the figure reports. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3.html (caption-numbers chunk; LSE and ILSE rows: D_bias 0.1875 both, E_perint 0.9500 both, F_accum 14.960 against 14.964, G_acf1 0.8560 against 0.8561, logL -262 +- 0.726 both)

The least-squares ratio needs the qualification the Diagnostics section attaches to it. The Gaussian
Fisher information of a least-squares fit presumes one constant residual variance, and the exact
simulator violates that premise, because the variance of a recorded interval rises and falls with the
open population. The factor of fifteen measures the size of that break; it is not an
information-matrix-equality failure of the same kind as the open-loop gating likelihood's, where the
predictive variance does follow the gating, and the two ratios are not read as two values of one
comparable quantity.

At lag one the autocorrelation of the per-interval score
on $k_{\mathrm{off}}$ is $-0.0043 \pm 0.0043$ for \texttt{IR}, indistinguishable from zero, against
$0.191 \pm 0.004$ for \texttt{R}, $0.864 \pm 0.0019$ for \texttt{NR} and $0.856 \pm 0.0022$ for
either least-squares arm. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_3.html (caption-numbers chunk; lag-one score ACF, k_off)
A positively correlated score makes the variance of its sum exceed the sum of the per-interval
informations, which is how a per-interval identity that holds for the gating-aware members accumulates into a failure. The
temporal correlation the two non-recursive members leave in their score is the local time correlation
of the current that \citet{milescu2005maximum} named as the dominant error of independent-interval

\subsection*{Where the calibrated member departs, and which moment carries it}

Figure~\ref{fig:limit} resolves the corner where the boundary-conditioned member departs, on a scale
Figure~\ref{fig:plane} cannot show. Finding that corner was half the object of the measurement, since
a diagnostic that never fires on the member it is applied to says nothing about what it can resolve.

The departure is two-sided within a single cell. At ten channels and $\widetilde{S} = 0.005$ the
reported error bar of the closing rate is too small by a factor of $1.62$ at the shortest interval
and that of the channel number too large by a factor of $0.65$, so one likelihood is over-confident
about a rate and conservative about a count in the same recording. % src: as the figure
The two parts of the decomposition answer to the acquisition interval in opposite directions: the
correlation part of the closing rate falls from $1.52$ to $1.27$ as the interval coarsens while the
per-sample part rises from $1.06$ to $1.19$, so sampling more coarsely buys back the memory and pays
for it in the per-sample non-Gaussianity. Both parts vanish by $\widetilde{S} = 1$,
where the instrumental noise has made the emission Gaussian enough that neither closure is strained. % src: as the figure

Two limits travel with the figure. The family applies both of its Gaussian approximations at every
step and the score sees only their product, so a departure measured here cannot be assigned to one of
them; separating them needs the microscopic recursion, a companion paper's subject. And the grid is
truncated where the effect grows: the closing rate's departure is still accelerating at the lowest
simulated noise, reading $1.22$, $1.37$ and $1.52$ over the last three noise steps at ten channels,
so the figure locates the onset and not the far side. % src: as the figure

Read upward at a fixed channel count the recording loses capabilities in a fixed order, which gives
five regions, numbered from the bottom. % src: papers/_program/nomenclature.md (regions of the usage map; 0 at the bottom, 4 at the top)
Region~0, below the lowest boundary, is where the Gaussian closure is itself misspecified and the
recording is not a macroscopic problem, the microscopic regime of the Theory seen on this plane. In region~1 the likelihood delivers the unitary current, the channel number and an honest
interval while least squares reports an interval several times too narrow; in region~2 the amplitude
pair stops being separable and the rates alone survive; in region~3 least squares is calibrated too
and buys the same answer more cheaply; in region~4 nothing useful is left. The lowest boundary is the
one with a negative slope, $-0.56$ in
$\mathrm{d}\log_{10}\mathrm{noise}/\mathrm{d}\log_{10}N_{\mathrm{ch}}$, because instrumental noise
makes the emission more Gaussian and so relaxes the occupancy closure while hurting everything else. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (fitted slopes)
Regions~1 to~4 sit inside the Gaussian regime, and what orders them is how much the data carry
rather than whether the closures hold. The telegraphic regime is a statement about $\Delta$, so it is
not a region of this plane; it is reached along the interval axis of Figure~\ref{fig:plane}.

The boundary at which the classical error bar becomes honest is one the mechanism predicts: the
gating variance grows with the channel count while the instrumental variance does not, so the ratio
of the two decides whether a window-ignoring fit is honest. Fitted in logarithms over four measured
crossings in each of two parameter directions, it runs as instrumental noise proportional to channel
number, with slopes of $1.01$ and $1.03$, a measured slope rather than an assumed scaling. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (crossings at noise label 164 and 130 at N_ch 10, 1.28e3 and 1.53e3 at 100, 1.54e4 and 1.51e4 at 1000, 1.62e5 and 1.59e5 at 10^4, channel-number and closing-rate directions)

The ordering of the boundaries is the result the map exists to state: there is no region of the
measured plane in which the amplitude still carries information and the classical error bar is
simultaneously trustworthy, the two being separated by one to three decades of noise everywhere
measured. The scope of that statement has to be stated with it: the classical arm here is least
squares on the deterministic mean current with the unitary current held fixed, in its four-parameter
configuration. It is not the variance-mean regression, this paper does not benchmark non-stationary
fluctuation analysis, and nothing here should be read as a measured comparison against it.

Minimising the corrected standard error over the whole noise sweep, the channel number is unreachable
below about $17$ channels and the opening rate below about $52$ at this interval, while the unitary
current and the closing rate have no floor within the simulated range. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (reachability floors)

The evidence is thinner than the picture, and the subsection says so rather than leaving it to a
caption. The two boundaries at which least squares becomes honest, and the closure floor, are each
fitted through four measured crossings. The channel count at which the third region opens spans $120$
to $1368$ depending only on which criterion is used. The lower vertex is extrapolated to
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}$ between $3$ and $9$, below the simulated floor of ten channels, and the ten cells
that would pin it have not been run. All the boundaries are drawn at one acquisition interval, the
ribbons showing where each moves as the interval is swept from $0.01$ to $1$ in units of $\tau$. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_6.html (region 3 opens at N_ch 120-1368 over the two criteria; map closes from below at N_ch 3-9, extrapolated)
