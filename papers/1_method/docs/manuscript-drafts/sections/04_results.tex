A recursive likelihood cannot be checked against a record by eye: it predicts each sample from the
samples before it, so it follows the record whether or not the uncertainty it reports is the
uncertainty it delivers. What follows measures it against least squares, on recordings simulated
exactly from the process every member approximates.

\subsection*{Recovery at one design cell}

Figure~\ref{fig:clouds} turns the ladder into a statement about reported uncertainty at one cell,
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}=100$, $\widetilde{S} = 0.01$, $\widetilde{\Delta} = 0.1$.

The interval average is what centres the estimate, and the roster isolates that step twice, one flag
apart in each family: \texttt{LSE} against \texttt{ILSE}, and \texttt{NR} against \texttt{INR}. In
the kinetic pair every member lands within $0.04$ in $\log_{10}$ of the truth, and both times the
interval average takes a factor of two to three off the offset in $k_{\mathrm{on}}$, while recursion
on its own does the opposite: the recursive instantaneous filter is the least centred member of the
pair at $-0.040$, worse than the open-loop member it is built from, and only \texttt{IR}, which has
both, has no offset resolved from zero in either coordinate. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_first_moment.R (per-parameter table; 1000 estimates, 2000-resample percentile bootstrap, type 6. k_on: LSE -0.0148, ILSE -0.0069, NR -0.0210, INR -0.0077, R -0.0401, MR -0.0310, VR -0.0021 unresolved, IR +0.0015 unresolved. k_off: MR -0.0090, VR -0.0123, NR +0.0104, the rest at or under 0.003)

In the amplitude and noise directions the same split costs an order of magnitude more, and there the
displacement has a mechanism the panel can be read for. The unitary current comes out low by between $0.103$ and
$0.228$ in $\log_{10}$ for \texttt{NR}, \texttt{VR}, \texttt{R} and \texttt{MR}, a factor of $0.59$ at
the worst, against $0.028$ for \texttt{INR} and nothing resolved for \texttt{IR}, and the channel
number comes out high by almost exactly the same amount, while every member recovers the product
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}\,i$ that the mean current determines to within $0.023$. % src: same script (amplitude-split table: bias in log10 i, in log10 N_ch and in their sum, per member). The per-member series was retired to a range on 2026-08-10 and is kept here: log10 i bias MR -0.228, R -0.156, VR -0.117, NR -0.103, INR -0.028.
What separates the two factors is the gating variance, which carries a second power of the unitary
current, so a member with the wrong interval variance splits a well-determined product wrongly. The
noise level takes the same shortfall: \texttt{NR} models no interval-mean conductance variance, and
the fit absorbs what is missing in the one free parameter that can take it, landing its noise
estimate $0.333$ in $\log_{10}$ above the truth, a factor of $2.2$, against every other member within
$0.04$. Conditioning on the interval instead of on an instant, and adding nothing else, returns it to
$0.005$.

The open marker in each panel is the truth displaced by the predicted bias, and that comparison is
partial. On $k_{\mathrm{off}}$ the prediction falls inside the sampling interval of the measured
displacement for all eight members; in the amplitude directions it carries the sign and $71$ to
$85\%$ of the size for \texttt{NR}, \texttt{R}, \texttt{MR} and \texttt{VR}, and it over-states
\texttt{NR}'s noise offset by a factor of $1.8$. % src: same script (predicted-against-measured table, with the bootstrap interval of the measured value)
The first moment is diagnosed to within a fifth rather than reproduced, which is what a first-order
prediction at the truth can do for a cloud fitted in groups of ten; the quantitative claim of this
section is the second moment.

In size, the reported covariance disagrees with the cloud by an order of magnitude at the foot of
the ladder and by nothing at its top. Averaged geometrically over the six pairs the four informative
parameters form, the ellipse-area ratio reads $11.41$ for the open-loop member, $1.17$ for the
recursive instantaneous one and $1.01$ for the boundary-conditioned one, and on the joint
four-parameter ellipse $154.65$, $1.56$ and $1.12$: a likelihood with no recursion under-reports the
area of the parameter distribution by a factor of ten to fifteen, and the recursive members are wrong
by tens of percent. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_2.Rmd (diagnostics chunk, empirical over Fisher; per-pair ranges are in the caption)
% "width" -> "area" 2026-08-06: the quantity is sqrt(det), so it is an area ratio and a factor of 11
% in it is a factor of 3.4 in a per-direction error bar. The abstract quotes the same number and
% needs the same check (D-4 §3 confirms the abstract's figure IS the ellipse-area ratio).
The fall between them is not monotone, and this is where the ladder stops being an ordering: on the
kinetic pair \texttt{MR} reads $1.97$ and \texttt{VR} reads $2.19$ against $1.32$ for the recursive
instantaneous filter, before \texttt{IR} returns to $1.02$, and the amplitude and noise pairs run the
same way. % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_2.Rmd (diagnostics chunk; amplitude pair 1.09/1.53/1.77/1.00, noise pair 1.06/1.29/1.43/0.99)
Conditioning on one endpoint costs calibration rather than buying it, in every pair and in both
directions of the variance switch; what recovers it is the second endpoint entering the gain
(Appendix~\ref{app:members}).

The interval average acts on the second moment too: \texttt{INR} improves on the open-loop member in
every pair, most on the noise pair, from $8.84$ to $3.28$, % src: projects/eLife_2025/figures/paper_both/figure_2.Rmd (diagnostics chunk; kinetic 14.83 to 12.59, amplitude 10.04 to 9.00, noise 8.84 to 3.28)
while the same step inside the least-squares family buys nothing in the one pair both arms carry,
the kinetic area ratio reading $14.73$ for \texttt{LSE} against $14.77$ for \texttt{ILSE} though
their first-moment offsets differ by a factor of two. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_shape_and_floor.R (kinetic pair, area column)
Averaging is worth a great deal to a likelihood that also models the gating variance and next to
nothing to one that does not.

A size is not a diagnosis, because the distortion also has a shape. Resizing the reported ellipse to
the empirical area, the most any single factor applied to an error bar can do, does not land it on
the empirical one. The spread of the two distortion eigenvalues runs from $1.0$ to $3.2$ across the
panels, against a finite-sample floor of at least $1.08$, the $95$th percentile of the same statistic
when a thousand estimates in two dimensions are drawn from a perfectly calibrated covariance, and a
lower bound because the reported covariance is itself estimated. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_shape_and_floor.R (per-pair size, shape and eigendirections; the floor from 20,000 null draws, median 1.038 and 95th percentile 1.081 at p=2, n=1000)
In the worst panel, \texttt{INR} in the noise pair, the two numbers read $3.3$ and $3.2$ and factor
into a variance ratio of $10.5$ along one direction and $1.03$ along the other, and the calibrated
direction is the noise level itself, within a tenth of a degree of that axis. The member that
repairs the noise estimate reports the noise uncertainty exactly right and the channel-number
uncertainty with a standard error $3.2$ times too small, so the single factor a scalar correction
would apply, $3.3$, is right for neither. The alignment is not particular to that member: in the
same pair \texttt{MR} and \texttt{VR} read $1.04$ and $1.02$ along the noise axis against $1.59$ and
$2.02$ across it. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_shape_and_floor.R (per-pair eigendirections, noise pair)

The distortion is removable, provided the correction is a matrix. Replacing
each reported covariance by the sandwich returns every gating-aware member of the ladder to within
about a tenth of one, with every parameter pair between $0.87$ and $1.07$, and it is the
least-squares family alone that it leaves short, at $1.65$ and $1.63$ for the two arms. % src: papers/1_method/decisions/D-4_ranking_verdict.md §1a (emp/corrected column) ; papers/1_method/decisions/recompute/fig2_shape_and_floor.R (corrected column: LSE 1.65, ILSE 1.63, NR 1.07, INR 1.05, R 0.96, MR 0.93, VR 0.97, IR 1.01 in the kinetic pair)
The two arms fail it by the same amount while differing in every first-moment quantity, which puts
the failure in what they share and not in the interval average. In coverage terms, the nominal $95\%$
region of \texttt{IR} holds $0.914$ of the estimates under its own Fisher covariance and $0.947$ to
$0.949$ under the sandwich, over a thousand fits in six parameters at group size $100$, at
$N_{\mathrm{ch}}=10$ and $\widetilde{S} = 0.005$ (Figure~\ref{fig:clouds}--figure supplement 1).

Both least-squares arms carry a column in the kinetic pair and none in the other two, for reasons of
identifiability rather than coverage: their grid configuration holds the unitary current and the

$\log_{10}$, but with the unitary current pinned at its true value that estimate is the mean current
under another name. What this figure measures is the separation of the two factors, the harder half
and the one the gating variance carries.

Region~0, below the lowest boundary, is where the Gaussian closure is itself misspecified and the
recording is not a macroscopic problem, the microscopic regime of the Theory seen on this plane. In region~1 the likelihood delivers the unitary current, the channel number and an honest
interval while least squares reports an interval several times too narrow; in region~2 the amplitude
pair stops being separable and the rates alone survive; in region~3 least squares is calibrated too
